Abliteration Lens · the built-in strips

Strips

The 23 strips built into Abliteration Lens, each with the help page it opens on the desk: what it does, an animated figure of what its code computes, GPT-2 small's text at the settings the figure walks through (prompt “What matters about the archive is”, seed 42), and its parameters.

These strips are the Lens's own code, so only the Lens runs them. The VAUs, such as vShrink and vSparsify from VAU's Core Collection, are Vector Abliteration Units: plug-ins that follow a standard any host can implement, documented on the VAU site.

Strips on the model

They change the forward pass: a component's output, the stream, the weights, the order of the blocks, the input.

steer layer=6 dir=negation scale=4 adds scale × a direction to the residual stream at every token (contrastive activation addition, as in Vectorscope's Grammar Steering). A negative scale pushes the other way.

Project out

project

Removes or reflects the stream's component along a direction.

Example line: project layer=all dir=negation mode=remove

Parameters

ParameterRangeWhat it does
Layer layera layer: 6, 3-7, or allThe residual stream it acts on: 0 is the embeddings, l the output of block l − 1; all, a range (3-7) or a list (1,4) work too.
Direction dira directionThe direction whose part of the stream is taken away or turned round, by name; only its direction counts, not its length.
Mode moderemove, reflect
default remove		remove takes the part along the direction away; reflect turns it round, so it points the other way.
To tozero, mean
default zero		zero measures the part from nothing; mean measures it from the stream's corpus mean, so remove sets it to the ordinary amount.
Directions k1 to 16
default 1		How many directions go at once: 1 is the direction alone, more adds the next principal directions of what it was made from.

Built into Abliteration Lens · backend/abliteration/interventions.py

More

project layer=all dir=negation mode=remove removes the part of the residual stream that points along a direction, leaving the rest: the model then cannot carry that direction at those layers (directional ablation). This is the probe for the negation deficit: can a direction be taken away or turned round, and what happens to "not" when it is?

mode=remove
takes the part away.
mode=reflect
reverses it, so what pointed one way along the direction now points the other way.
to=mean
measures the part from the stream's mean at that layer over the reference corpus, so removing it sets it to the ordinary amount rather than to none (mean-referenced, or affine, erasure, as OBLITERATUS offers it): a gentler cut.
k=4
removes four directions at once, a subspace: the direction itself, then the next principal directions of the differences it was made from (its texts' last-token streams for a contrast, its tokens against the corpus for a passage, its high positions against its low for a decision direction). A direction from one word has only itself.
A finding from the build. With the decision direction for negation on GPT-2 (four prompts, four seeds; negation words per 1,000, the original 23): removed on every layer, 0, with the texts collapsing; to=mean on every layer, 26, with the texts intact (−11.3 DUFS); at layer 10, to zero 0 (−12.9 DUFS), to the mean 8 (−22), k=4 8 with shorter texts (−12.1).

coarsen

Rounds one block's weights to fewer bits.

Parameters

More

coarsen block=5 bits=4 rounds the weight matrices of one block to 4-bit levels (or 8, 3, 2) and leaves the rest of the model at full precision: material grain as a local change.

Mix mix0 to 1
default 1		With a swap, how far the first word's embedding moves toward the second's instead: 0 its own, 1 the second's; the second word is left as it is.

More
embed swap=" not, also"
the model reads "not" with the embedding of "also" and the reverse, wherever they occur in the input, prompt and generated text alike. GPT-2 tokens usually begin with a space.
mix=0.3
with a swap, the first word moves toward the second instead of trading places with it: embed swap=" archive, museum" mix=0.3 reads "archive" with its embedding 30% of the way to "museum"'s, and leaves "museum" as it is. Mix 0 is neutral.

vocoder

Imposes another prompt's band levels on the stream being written.

Parameters

ParameterRangeWhat it does
Layer layera layer: 6, 3-7, or allThe one layer of the residual stream where the modulator's band levels are imposed: 0 is the embeddings, l the output of block l − 1.
Modulator promptThe storm broke over the seaThe modulator: the text whose band levels, measured on the unchanged model, are imposed on the text being written.
Bands bands2 to 64

More

Vocoder

vocoder layer=6 prompt="The storm broke over the sea" bands=16 mix=1 takes the prompt as a modulator. The stream at that layer over it (unchanged model, averaged over its tokens, less the corpus mean) is measured in bands of principal directions spaced logarithmically: narrow among the largest directions and wide in the tail, as a vocoder's bands are over frequency.

At each position of the text being written (the carrier), each band is rescaled to the modulator's size in that band, keeping the carrier's direction within it. It works like a Transplant done in bands. Mix 0 is neutral; the attention sink is left alone. On the archive prompt, mix 0.5 changes the text and keeps it fluent (−20.7 DUFS); mix 1 breaks it apart (−8.6).

Pan

pan block=all depth=4 pan=0.5 adds a bias by distance to the attention scores in the chosen blocks: for a query at position q and an earlier key at k, −pan × depth × (q − k) / q.

pan above 0
moves attention toward the most recent tokens.
pan below 0
toward the start (the prompt).
pan 0
is neutral.

It registers its own attention function for the run (the model's eager attention with the bias added) and puts the model's back after. At pan 1, depth 4, head 5.1's average look-back falls from 19 tokens to 3, and GPT-2 repeats itself ("is is is").

Rewrite

rewrite

A rank-one MLP edit that makes the model write a target after a prompt (inspired by ROME).

Example line: rewrite block=5 prompt="The Eiffel Tower is located in the city of" subject="Eiffel Tower" target=" Rome" amount=1

Parameters

ParameterRangeWhat it does
Block block0 to 47
default 5		The block whose MLP is edited (GPT-2 small has blocks 0 to 11).
Prompt promptThe Eiffel Tower is located in the city ofA sentence that leads up to the fact, ending where the target should follow.
Subject subjectEiffel TowerThe words in the prompt the fact is about; the edit is keyed to their last token.
Target target" Rome"What the model should write after the prompt, usually with a leading space.
Save as patch savea nameA name to also save the edit under in data/patches, for the Patch strip to load.
Amount amount0 to 2
default 1		How much of the edit is added: 0 changes nothing, 1 is the fitted edit, 2 doubles it.

Built into Abliteration Lens · backend/abliteration/interventions.py

More

Rewrite

rewrite block=5 prompt="The Eiffel Tower is located in the city of" subject="Eiffel Tower" target=" Rome" edits one fact, inspired by ROME (Meng, Bau, Andonian and Belinkov 2022).

The MLP's key at the subject's last token is read over the prompt and versions of it with a corpus passage's opening in front. A change to that MLP's output at that token is found by gradient descent so that the model writes the target. It is then made a rank-one change to the MLP's output projection through the covariance of the MLP's keys over the reference corpus, so that the subject's key gains the change and other keys as little as their likeness to it allows.

The fit takes some seconds the first time and is kept. It acts as a hook (amount 0 is neutral), and save=name also writes it as a patch.

A finding from the build. On GPT-2, at block 5, the chance of " Rome" after the prompt rises from 0.005 to 0.81; after "The Eiffel Tower is in" from 0.002 to 0.16; after "The Louvre is located in the city of" it stays at 0.006. The edit takes on its own prompt, carries over in part to paraphrases, and leaves other subjects nearly as they were.

Patch

patch file=name scale=1 loads data/patches/name.safetensors, whose tensors, keyed by the model's parameter names, are changes to add (or, with the file's metadata kind=replace, weights to move toward), scaled, for the run; the weights are put back after. Scale 0 is neutral.

A model edited elsewhere can be read against itself unedited, with the meters, the loudness and Leave one out.

Position bend

position

Shifts, scrambles or freezes the position embeddings the model reads.

Example line: position mode=shift k=200

Parameters

ParameterRangeWhat it does
Mode modeshift, scramble, freeze
default shift		shift reads position p + k, scramble a fixed random position for each, freeze position k for every token.
Seed seed0 to 9999
default 0		The seed of scramble's random positions (the run's seed if not given); shift and freeze ignore it.
Positions k−512 to 512
default 0		How many positions shift moves each token on (0 changes nothing), or the one position freeze reads; scramble ignores it.

More

position mode=shift k=200 changes which position embedding each token is read with.

shift
reads position p + k (held within the table), so the model takes itself to be further into a text than it is. Shift by 0 is neutral.
scramble
reads a fixed random other position for each (drawn from the seed, the same in every pass), so word order is lost.
freeze
reads position k for every token.

It needs learned position embeddings, which GPT-2 has; Pythia and Llama-style models use rotary positions and refuse it. On the archive prompt a shift of 600 collapses GPT-2 into "all and all a all all" (−2.8 DUFS); scramble and freeze lose the order (about −6 DUFS).

Sampler strips

They change how the next token is chosen from the model's probabilities.

Contrast

contrast

Divides the diverged model's next-token probabilities by the original's to a power.

Example line: contrast alpha=1 keep=0.1

Parameters

ParameterRangeWhat it does
Keep keep0.001 to 1
default 0.1		Only tokens with at least this share of the diverged model's top chance can be chosen.
Alpha alpha0 to 3
default 1		How strongly the original model's chances are divided out; 0 changes nothing.

Built into Abliteration Lens · backend/abliteration/generate.py

More

contrast alpha=1 keep=0.1 acts at the sampler, at every step. It runs the original model on the same text as well (every hook is switched off for that pass) and samples from log pdiverged − α log poriginal, over the tokens the diverged model finds plausible (at least keep times its most probable token's probability); the rest are ruled out (contrastive decoding, inspired by Li et al. 2022).

Words the chain made likelier gain; words both models favour equally lose. It enlarges the divergence itself. α 0 is neutral.

It cannot run beside a strip that changes the model itself (Coarsen, Reorder, Exit), since the original model must be read in the same step. Like the other sampler bends it does not count in the loudness, which reads the models' distributions.

chorus

Writes with several detuned copies of the model, averaging their probabilities.

Parameters

chorus layer=6 voices=3 sigma=0.3 writes with several slightly detuned copies of the model at once. Each voice is the chain with noise added to the stream at that layer (scaled to the stream's size, as Noise's is) from its own seed, run with its own cache. At each step the next token is drawn from the average of the dry chain's and the voices' probabilities.

Voices 0 is neutral. Each voice is a further pass at every step, so three voices take about four times as long. Effects that keep earlier positions (vLookback, vAverage, vQuantise, vRepeat) share their memory between the voices.

Meters

They read the model and leave it unchanged, so the diverged text is the same with them or without them.

tap

The words the stream points to at this layer (the logit lens).

Example line: tap layer=6

Parameters

ParameterRangeWhat it does
Layer layerone layer: 0 to the lastThe layer whose stream is read through the final norm and the output layer: 12 is the model's own output, 0 the embedding.

Built into Abliteration Lens · backend/abliteration/generate.py

More

Meters register no hook while the two texts are written. After the run, one more forward pass over each text, teacher-forced, fills them in: over the original text with the meters alone, over the diverged text with the whole chain. A meter placed after a strip on the same layer reads the stream after that strip; on other layers the order on the desk makes no difference.

tap layer=6
the logit lens. The stream at that layer goes through the final norm and the output layer as if it were the last, and the strip shows the four words it points to most, and the rank and probability there of the word the model went on to write. At the top layer (12 for GPT-2) the tap is the model's own output. In the middle layers of GPT-2 the logit lens is rough: it shows what the output layer would make of the stream, which the later layers may still change.
meter layer=6
the stream's size (its L2 norm) at each step.
gauge layer=6 dir=negation

Up to the point where the texts part, the two runs read the same tokens, so a difference between the readings there comes from the chain alone; after it, the texts differ too.

meter

The size of the stream at this layer, token by token.

Example line: meter layer=6

Parameters

ParameterRangeWhat it does
Layer layerone layer: 0 to the lastThe layer whose stream's size (its L2 norm) is shown at each token: 0 is the embedding, 12 the last block's output.

Built into Abliteration Lens · backend/abliteration/generate.py

More

Meters register no hook while the two texts are written. After the run, one more forward pass over each text, teacher-forced, fills them in: over the original text with the meters alone, over the diverged text with the whole chain. A meter placed after a strip on the same layer reads the stream after that strip; on other layers the order on the desk makes no difference.

tap layer=6
the logit lens. The stream at that layer goes through the final norm and the output layer as if it were the last, and the strip shows the four words it points to most, and the rank and probability there of the word the model went on to write. At the top layer (12 for GPT-2) the tap is the model's own output. In the middle layers of GPT-2 the logit lens is rough: it shows what the output layer would make of the stream, which the later layers may still change.
