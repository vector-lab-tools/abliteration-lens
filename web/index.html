Probabilities 

    
Each token is coloured by its probability under the model named above the panel: no colour at 70% or more, deepening as the probability falls (LLMbench's scale). Each text is shown under its own model and under the other: the diverged text under the original model measures how far the bending has pushed the prose off the trained distribution; the original text under the diverged model shows what the diverged model now finds improbable. Both models are the same weights, with and without the chain's hooks; the scores are never pooled. Sampler bends leave the model untouched, so for them the two models are the same.

Control band 

    
The original is generated again with n other seeds (the recipe's seed + 1 to + n), and each reseed is measured against the original at the recipe's seed in the same way as the diverged text. A diverged value inside the reseeds' range is reported as inside the band: the chain moved the text no further than a change of seed does.

Directions for Steer and Project out

    
A direction is a vector in the residual stream, made here from two lists of texts, from one word, or from a sentence or passage against the reference corpus. It is a product of what it was made from. Finding where a direction lives, with its PCA and separability charts, is Vectorscope's work; here a direction is used. Directions are kept per model in data/directions/; a recipe carries the ones it uses.

Each positive word goes with the negative word in the same place, and each pair is written once for every ending, so the lists line up pair by pair, as the PCA methods read them. repeng builds its contrast sets this way, with endings cut from the model's own writing.

          

          
The difference between the two lists' mean residual vectors at their last token, at every layer (Vectorscope's method). Whatever separates the lists is in it. The PCA methods (inspired by repeng) read the lists as pairs, line by line: the first principal component of the pairs' differences, or of both lists centred on each pair's midpoint, signed so that most positives lie above their negatives and scaled to the difference of means along it.

        

        

          
          
A direction made from others, layer by layer: each term is a direction's name, or a number, * and a name. The directions must belong to the same model. A word's direction, which has one vector, counts at every layer.

        

        

          
          
The word as the output layer writes it: the word's row of the unembedding matrix. Project it out at the top layer (layer=12 for GPT-2) and the word drops out of the likely next tokens while the rest of the text stays fluent; at every layer it also changes how the model reads. Steer with a negative scale to push away from it. A word of several tokens uses its first. The layer box is not used. To take away what the model holds after reading a word, make a direction from a sentence instead, with the word in it.

        

        

          
          
How the residual stream on these texts differs from the stream on the reference corpus: the mean over the texts' tokens minus the mean over the corpus's tokens, at every layer. Several lines are averaged. Blank layer: where the texts stand furthest from the corpus.

        

        

          
          
          
          

          

          
Where the model is about to write one of the words. The model reads the reference corpus (and any contexts above) and at every position gives a chance that the next token is one of them; the direction at each layer is the mean residual stream at the positions where that chance is highest minus the mean where it is lowest. It is read where the model decides, before the word, as Arditi et al. read the refusal direction at the end of the instruction. A contrast set is read at the end of each sentence, after the construction, so its direction marks having written it. Project this one out, or Abliterate with it, to stop the model writing the words; Steer with it to make them likelier. Each word counts by its first token.
